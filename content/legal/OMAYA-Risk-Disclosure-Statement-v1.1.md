## 4. Risks of Digital Assets (Cryptocurrency)

- **Volatility.** Digital asset prices can be extremely volatile and can change dramatically in a short time, including to zero.
- **Liquidity.** Markets can become illiquid, making it difficult or impossible to buy, sell, or convert an asset at the price or time you want.
- **Irreversibility and wrong network.** Blockchain transactions are generally **final and cannot be reversed, cancelled, or recovered**. Sending assets to a wrong or incompatible address — or through an **unsupported or incorrect network** (for example, confusing networks such as TRC20, ERC20, BEP20, or others for the same asset) — or sending **unsupported tokens**, can result in **permanent loss**, and OMAYA may be unable or unwilling to recover them.
- **Technology and network risk.** Networks may experience congestion, delays, forks, reorganisations, or attacks (including "51%" attacks), and **smart contracts and protocols may contain bugs or be exploited**, including hacks, liquidity-pool drains, and protocol failures.
- **Network fees.** Blockchain network fees can rise suddenly and unpredictably and may reduce the final amount you receive.
- **Market integrity.** Digital asset markets are relatively new and may be affected by **market manipulation, wash trading, spoofing, pump-and-dump activity, insider dealing, fake volume, social-media hype, low liquidity, and conflicts of interest** among issuers, exchanges, liquidity providers, or other participants.
- **Issuer or project failure.** A digital asset may lose value, including to zero, because the issuer, protocol, or project **fails, is hacked, is abandoned, is fraudulent (for example, a "rug pull"), becomes insolvent, loses liquidity or its community, suffers a smart-contract failure, or becomes subject to regulatory action**.
- **Security.** Digital assets are a target for theft, hacking, fraud, and key compromise. Loss of credentials, private keys, or recovery phrases can cause permanent loss of access.
