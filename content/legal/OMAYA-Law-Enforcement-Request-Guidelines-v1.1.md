# Law Enforcement Request Guidelines

**OMAYA EXPRESS EXCHANGE**

**Effective Date:** 31 May 2026  
**Last Updated:** 31 May 2026  
**Version:** 1.1

---

These Guidelines explain how **OMAYA EXPRESS EXCHANGE** (trading as **OMAYA.io**, "**OMAYA**," "**we**," "**us**," or "**our**") responds to requests from law enforcement, regulatory, and other government authorities ("**Authorities**") for customer information, the preservation of records, and the freezing or restriction of accounts or assets.

They are intended as practical guidance for Authorities and to inform our users. They are **not legal advice**, do not form part of any contract, and do **not** expand, limit, or waive any rights, obligations, defences, or objections available to OMAYA, our users, or any other person under applicable law.

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

These Guidelines apply to requests directed to OMAYA that relate to our Services, including requests for:

- customer or account information;
- the preservation of records;
- the freezing, blocking, or restriction of accounts, transactions, or assets; and
- other lawful assistance in connection with an investigation or legal proceeding.

## 2. Governing Law and Cooperation with Authorities

OMAYA is incorporated in the Federal Republic of Somalia and handles requests in accordance with applicable Somali law and our legal obligations. We cooperate with the **Financial Reporting Center (FRC)**, the **Central Bank of Somalia**, Somali law enforcement, courts, and other competent Somali Authorities. We may also respond to requests from foreign Authorities where there is a proper and lawful basis to do so (see Section 14). We will only disclose information, or take action, where we are permitted or required to do so by applicable law and valid legal process.

Legal process should be served **directly on OMAYA** at our Mogadishu headquarters or through the contact channel in Section 17, and **not** on our cloud, hosting, or other infrastructure providers. Customer data is encrypted at rest, and we will contest or seek to redirect any request for customer data that is served on an underlying infrastructure provider rather than on OMAYA.

## 3. How to Submit a Request

Requests should be submitted by an authorised official to **lawenforcement@omaya.io**.

To allow us to identify the relevant records and respond, a request should:

- be made on **official letterhead**, signed by an authorised official, and sent from an **official government email domain**;
- identify the **issuing Authority** and the **name, title, and contact details** of the requesting official;
- state the **legal basis and authority** for the request and any case or reference number;
- specify the **information sought** and the **time period** to which it relates; and
- identify the customer or account as precisely as possible — for example, by account email address, customer name, OMAYA account identifier, transaction reference, or wallet address.

Requests that are vague, overly broad, or that do not identify a specific customer, account, or transaction may be declined or returned for clarification.

**Response times.** We aim to acknowledge receipt of routine requests within **5 business days**. Full production of records typically takes **30 to 45 days**, depending on the volume and complexity of the request and the legal process involved. Emergency requests are handled on an expedited basis (see Section 6).

## 4. Valid Legal Process Required

We require **valid legal process** appropriate to the request. Different categories of information or action may require different levels of legal process, and more sensitive information or actions (such as the disclosure of detailed records, the content of communications, or the freezing or seizure of assets) generally require a court order, warrant, or directive from a competent Authority. We review each request for **validity, legal sufficiency, and specificity**, and we may **object to, narrow, seek to modify, or decline** a request that is improper, overbroad, defective, or not supported by adequate legal authority.

## 5. Categories of Records

Subject to applicable law, valid legal process, and what we actually hold, the information we may be able to provide includes:

- **subscriber and identity information** (such as name, date of birth, and contact details collected at registration);
- **identity-verification (KYC) records**;
- **account information** (such as account status and history);
- **transaction records** (such as deposits, withdrawals, exchanges, swaps, transfers, and associated wallet addresses);
- **device, IP, and login records**; and
- **communications and P2P records** held by us, such as support correspondence and P2P dispute evidence.

We provide only the information we hold and that the legal process lawfully requires. The personal data we collect is described in our **Privacy Policy**.

**Partner broker records.** Where OMAYA acts as a local agent for a third-party foreign exchange Broker, OMAYA does **not** hold and **cannot produce** that Broker's internal trading records — including trading-ledger data, order and execution records, margin-call histories, or MT4/MT5 terminal or Expert Advisor (EA) logs. Such records are held by the Broker, and Authorities must direct separate legal process to the relevant Broker entity in its own jurisdiction.

**Detailed technical logs.** We provide standard transaction histories (such as deposits, withdrawals, exchanges, swaps, transfers, and final executions on our own Platform). Raw server-level logs, detailed event logs, or similar technical data that we hold may require a specific and narrowly framed request, may take additional time to extract, and may be subject to the cost reimbursement described in Section 12.

## 6. Emergency Disclosure Requests

Where a request involves an **imminent risk of death or serious physical harm**, we operate an expedited process. An emergency request should be clearly marked as such, sent to **lawenforcement@omaya.io**, and include a good-faith explanation of the emergency, the harm at risk, and how the requested information will help prevent it. Where permitted by applicable law, we may voluntarily disclose information to an Authority if we believe in good faith that doing so is necessary to prevent such harm.

## 7. Preservation Requests

On receipt of a valid preservation request, we will take reasonable steps to **preserve relevant records that we hold** for a period of **90 days**, which may be extended once on a further valid request, pending service of appropriate legal process. A preservation request does not by itself require us to disclose any information.

## 8. Asset Freezing, Blocking, and Seizure

Where required by applicable law, a court order, or a directive from a competent Authority, and to meet our sanctions and AML/CFT obligations, we may **freeze, block, restrict, or decline to process** accounts, transactions, or assets, and report the matter. The treatment and availability of frozen or restricted assets is subject to applicable law and the relevant legal process.

We distinguish between a **freeze or blocking order**, which locks or restricts an account or assets on the OMAYA Platform, and a **forfeiture or seizure order**, which requires the transfer of assets out of OMAYA's control. We will act on a valid freeze order to lock or restrict assets, but we will **only transfer digital assets to a government-designated wallet, or otherwise release assets to an Authority, on receipt of a final, enforceable forfeiture or seizure order from a competent court** — not on the basis of a preliminary or investigative freeze request.

## 9. Notice to Account Holders

Our general approach is to be transparent with our users. We may **notify affected users** of a request for their information so that they may seek to protect their interests, **unless** we are prohibited from doing so by law, a court order, or a non-disclosure or "tipping-off" obligation, or where we believe in good faith that notice would be counterproductive — for example, where it could endanger a person, enable the destruction of evidence, or harm an investigation. **Where we are legally prohibited from giving notice, we will not do so, and we may be unable to confirm whether a request exists.**

We expect a non-disclosure or gag order to specify a **defined expiration date** (for example, 90 to 180 days). Where a non-disclosure order has no expiry or is of indefinite duration, we reserve the right to seek clarification or to challenge it before a competent Somali court, so that a user's right to be informed is not suspended indefinitely.

## 10. Authentication and Fraud Prevention

We take steps to **verify the authenticity** of requests and the authority of the requester. We may decline to act on, or seek further verification of, any request that we cannot authenticate or that appears irregular, fraudulent, or improper. Fraudulent requests for customer data are taken seriously and may themselves be reported to the Authorities.

## 11. Data Protection and Minimisation

We disclose personal data only to the extent permitted or required by applicable law and valid legal process, and we seek to limit disclosure to the information that is actually requested and necessary. Our handling of personal data is described in our **Privacy Policy**, and our financial-crime controls are summarised in our **AML/KYC Policy**.

## 12. Cost Reimbursement

Where permitted by law, we may seek reimbursement of the reasonable costs we incur in responding to a request, particularly for voluminous, complex, or repeated requests.

## 13. Reservation of Rights

Nothing in these Guidelines waives any right, objection, privilege, or defence available to OMAYA or our users, or constitutes consent to jurisdiction. We reserve the right to **object to, challenge, narrow, or decline** any request, and to require that requests follow the proper legal channels.

## 14. Foreign and Cross-Border Requests

Authorities outside Somalia should generally proceed through recognised legal channels — such as a **mutual legal assistance treaty (MLAT), letters rogatory, or other channels recognised under Somali law** — unless another lawful basis for the request exists. We may decline to act on a direct request from a foreign Authority that is not supported by an appropriate legal basis.

## 15. No Legal Advice and No Third-Party Rights

These Guidelines are provided for information only. They are not legal advice, do not create any contractual or other rights or obligations, and do not limit the legal requirements that apply to OMAYA or to any Authority.

## 16. Changes to These Guidelines

We may update these Guidelines from time to time. We will post the updated version and update the "Last Updated" date.
